# LITERATURE SURVEY
## Project Title: Exam Regulation Assistant Using RAG with Guardrails

### 1. Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used to develop intelligent question-answering systems. However, traditional AI chatbots may sometimes provide incorrect, outdated or misleading information. This is a major concern when students need accurate information about examination rules and academic regulations.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines information retrieval with AI-based text generation. It allows a system to search relevant information from external documents before generating an answer. Guardrails provide additional controls to improve the reliability, relevance and safety of generated responses.

The proposed Exam Regulation Assistant uses these technologies to help students obtain accurate information from official examination regulations.

### 2. Review of Existing Research Papers

**Paper 1: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks (2020)**

Authors: Patrick Lewis et al.

This research introduced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which combines a retrieval system with a language generation model. The approach allows AI models to access external knowledge sources while answering questions. It provides the foundation for developing document-based question-answering systems.

**Relevance to our project:** This paper provides the fundamental concept for retrieving examination regulations and generating answers based on the retrieved information.

**Paper 2: A Systematic Literature Review of Retrieval-Augmented Generation: Techniques, Metrics, and Challenges (2025)**

This research reviews different RAG techniques, evaluation methods and challenges. It discusses how retrieval quality, information accuracy and system performance affect the effectiveness of RAG-based applications.

**Relevance to our project:** It helps us understand how to improve document retrieval and evaluate the accuracy of answers generated by our examination assistant.

**Paper 3: Hallucination Mitigation for Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models: A Review (2025)**

Authors: Wan Zhang and Jing Zhang.

This research focuses on hallucinations in RAG-based language models. It explains that incorrect responses can occur due to poor document retrieval, irrelevant information and limitations in the generation process. It also discusses techniques for detecting and reducing hallucinations.

**Relevance to our project:** This research supports the need for guardrails that check whether generated answers are supported by official examination documents.

**Paper 4: Safeguarding Large Language Models: A Survey (2025)**

This research examines safety mechanisms and guardrails used in Large Language Models. It discusses methods for controlling AI responses, improving reliability and reducing undesirable outputs.

**Relevance to our project:** It provides background for implementing input validation, output checking and restrictions on unsupported answers.

### 3. Existing System

Traditional methods of accessing examination regulations mainly involve searching through PDF documents, college websites, notices and academic handbooks.

General-purpose AI chatbots can answer questions in natural language, but they may generate information that is not supported by the institution's official examination regulations.

The limitations of existing approaches include:

- Difficulty finding specific information in lengthy documents.
- Time-consuming manual searching.
- Possibility of receiving outdated information.
- AI-generated answers that may contain incorrect information.
- Lack of direct references to official examination rules.

### 4. Proposed System

The proposed Exam Regulation Assistant Using RAG with Guardrails is designed to provide students with a simple way to access examination-related information.

The system uses official examination documents as its knowledge source. When a student asks a question, the RAG pipeline retrieves relevant information and passes it to the language model to generate an understandable answer.

Guardrails are applied to check the relevance and support of the generated response. If the required information is not available in the documents, the system should inform the student rather than provide an unsupported answer.

The main features include:

- Document-based question answering.
- Retrieval of relevant examination regulations.
- Natural language responses.
- Guardrails for checking generated answers.
- References to supporting documents.
- Handling of questions outside the available knowledge base.

### 5. Research Gap

From the reviewed literature, RAG has been widely studied for knowledge-intensive question answering, and guardrails have been explored for improving the reliability of language models.

However, there is a practical opportunity to combine these approaches into a focused assistant for institutional examination regulations.

The proposed project addresses this application-specific need by focusing on:

1. Retrieving information specifically from official examination documents.
2. Generating simple and understandable answers for students.
3. Reducing unsupported or misleading responses through guardrails.
4. Providing references to the relevant examination documents.
5. Informing students when the required information is unavailable.

### 6. Comparison Between Existing and Proposed Systems

| Existing System | Proposed System |
|---|---|
| Manual searching of PDF documents | Automated document retrieval |
| General-purpose chatbot responses | Examination-specific answers |
| May provide unsupported information | Uses guardrails to check responses |
| Difficult to locate relevant rules | Retrieves relevant information |
| Limited document-based references | Provides supporting document references |

### 7. Conclusion

The literature survey shows that Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a useful approach for developing knowledge-based question-answering systems. Research on hallucination mitigation and LLM guardrails also highlights the importance of controlling and verifying AI-generated responses.

Based on these findings, the proposed Exam Regulation Assistant combines RAG and guardrails to create a domain-specific question-answering system for examination regulations. The system aims to reduce students' difficulties in finding academic rules, improve access to official information and provide more reliable answers.
